Uterine PRP Therapy for IVF: Restoring Thin Endometrium & Overcoming Implantation Failure

By Dr. Santosh Gupta November 18, 2026

One of the most disheartening experiences in an IVF journey is having top-quality, chromosomally normal blastocysts frozen in the laboratory, only to have your Frozen Embryo Transfer (FET) repeatedly cancelled because your uterine lining refuses to thicken.

For optimal embryo implantation, the endometrium must reach a minimum thickness of 7 mm with a distinct trilaminar (triple-line) ultrasonic pattern. In refractory cases—where the lining plateaus at 5 mm or 6 mm despite high-dose oral estradiol, transdermal patches, vaginal sildenafil, and pentoxifylline—the endometrium lacks vascularity and cellular proliferation.

Over the past five years, intrauterine Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) therapy has emerged as a groundbreaking, evidence-based biological treatment to regenerate compromised endometrial tissue and restore uterine receptivity.

What is Intrauterine Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP)?

Platelet-Rich Plasma is an autologous (derived from your own body) concentration of blood platelets suspended in a small volume of plasma. Blood is drawn from the patient's arm and processed through a specialized double-centrifugation protocol to isolate the platelet layer.

Platelets are natural biological reservoirs of essential tissue repair proteins, including Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF), Transforming Growth Factor-beta (TGF-β), Platelet-Derived Growth Factor (PDGF), and Epidermal Growth Factor (EGF). When infused directly into the uterine cavity, these growth factors stimulate neo-angiogenesis (new capillary formation), recruit endometrial stem cells, and trigger rapid mucosal cellular proliferation.

100% Autologous & Biocompatible

Because PRP is prepared entirely from your own blood, there is zero risk of allergic reaction, foreign body rejection, or infectious transmission.

Clinical Indications for Uterine PRP in Bengaluru

Refractory Thin Endometrium (<7mm)

Patients whose endometrium does not respond to conventional hormone replacement therapy (HRT) or mild natural cycle preparation.

Recurrent Implantation Failure (RIF)

Couples who have failed two or more transfers with top-quality blastocysts despite seemingly adequate lining measurements.

Post-Infection or Post-Curettage Damage

Endometrial scarring from vigorous D&C procedures, pelvic inflammatory disease, or healed genital tuberculosis.

Suboptimal Endometrial Blood Flow

Uterine arteries showing high resistance index (RI) and poor sub-endometrial vascularization on color Doppler ultrasound.

The Step-by-Step Uterine PRP Protocol

  • Preparation & Blood Draw: On Day 9 or 10 of an estrogen-primed FET cycle, approximately 15 to 20 mL of peripheral blood is collected from the patient.
  • Sterile Centrifugation: The blood is spun in an automated medical centrifuge under strict aseptic laboratory conditions to separate red blood cells and concentrate platelets by 400% to 500%.
  • Intrauterine Infusion: Using a thin, soft embryo transfer catheter, 0.5 to 1.0 mL of activated PRP is gently infused into the endometrial cavity under ultrasound guidance. The procedure takes under 5 minutes and is completely painless, requiring no sedation or anesthesia.
  • Ultrasound Assessment: Endometrial thickness, pattern, and radial artery blood flow are re-evaluated 48 to 72 hours later. If necessary, a second infusion is administered before progesterone commencement.

Clinical Evidence: What Does the Research Show?

A landmark meta-analysis published in the journal Fertility and Sterility analyzing over 1,000 IVF cycles demonstrated that intrauterine PRP administration in women with refractory thin endometrium resulted in a statistically significant increase in endometrial thickness (average gain of 1.2 to 2.1 mm) and a doubling of clinical pregnancy rates compared to controls.
