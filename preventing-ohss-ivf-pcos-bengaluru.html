Preventing OHSS in IVF: Modern Protocols for Safe Treatment with PCOS

By Dr. Santosh Gupta November 02, 2026

For women with Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) or an exceptionally high ovarian reserve (AMH > 4.5 ng/mL), undergoing IVF can provoke anxiety. Online searches frequently highlight stories of severe bloating, hospitalization, fluid accumulation in the abdomen, and Ovarian Hyperstimulation Syndrome (OHSS).

In the past, OHSS was a dreaded complication of fertility treatment. However, in modern advanced reproductive medicine, the clinical landscape has fundamentally transformed.

At Nova IVF Fertility in Bengaluru, we operate under a strict 'OHSS-Free Clinic' philosophy. Today, with antagonist stimulation, GnRH agonist triggers, and elective embryo vitrification, severe OHSS is virtually 100% preventable.

What Triggers OHSS?

OHSS is an exaggerated systemic response to gonadotropin ovarian stimulation. When dozens of follicles mature simultaneously, the ovaries release massive amounts of vasoactive chemicals—predominantly Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF).

VEGF dramatically increases vascular permeability, causing fluid to leak from blood vessels into peritoneal and pleural spaces, leading to ascites (abdominal swelling), hemoconcentration, and electrolyte imbalances. Crucially, OHSS is driven and amplified by hCG (Human Chorionic Gonadotropin).

The Pillars of Our OHSS-Free Protocol

1. GnRH Antagonist Protocols (No Agonist Down-Regulation)

We utilize flexible GnRH antagonist regimens (Cetrotide/Orgalutran) rather than long agonist protocols. Antagonist protocols allow precise, rapid suppression of premature LH surges with shorter stimulation duration and lower overall gonadotropin requirements.

2. GnRH Agonist Trigger (The Game-Changer)

Instead of triggering final oocyte maturation with traditional urinary or recombinant hCG (which lingers in the bloodstream for 8–10 days driving VEGF release), we trigger with a GnRH agonist (e.g., Decapeptyl or Lupride). The agonist induces an endogenous LH flare that clears within 24–36 hours, eliminating the prolonged stimulus that triggers OHSS.

3. Elective Freeze-All Strategy

Transferring a fresh embryo into a hyper-stimulated ovary is hazardous because an early pregnancy produces its own endogenous hCG, which can trigger late-onset severe OHSS. By vitrifying all blastocysts, we let the patient's ovaries return to baseline before scheduling a calm, natural or HRT frozen transfer.

4. Dopamine Agonist Support (Cabergoline)

In women with high follicle counts (>20 follicles), administering oral Cabergoline (0.5 mg daily for 8 days post-retrieval) selectively blocks VEGF receptor-2 phosphorylation, preventing capillary leakage.

Why PCOS Patients Actually Have Superb IVF Outcomes

Once the threat of OHSS is completely neutralized by modern protocols, women with PCOS are actually among the highest-success patient cohorts in IVF. Their plentiful ovarian reserve yields ample eggs, which translates into healthy blastocysts and multiple opportunities for pregnancy from a single retrieval.

If you have PCOS and have been hesitant to explore IVF due to fears of hyperstimulation, modern reproductive medicine has made treatment gentle, safe, and profoundly effective.
