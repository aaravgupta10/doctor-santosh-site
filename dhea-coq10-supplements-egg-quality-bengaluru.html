DHEA & CoQ10 Before IVF: Can Supplements Improve Egg Quality After 35?

By Dr. Santosh Gupta October 30, 2026

Walk into any online fertility forum, and you will see discussions buzzing with supplement stacks: CoQ10, DHEA, Inositol, Melatonin, PQQ, and Vitamin D. For women over 35 or those diagnosed with low AMH (Anti-Müllerian Hormone), the search for anything that can 'turn back the biological clock' is intense.

As an IVF specialist who has practiced reproductive endocrinology for over 25 years, I believe in balancing scientific honesty with therapeutic optimism. You cannot create new eggs from scratch—a woman is born with her lifetime supply. However, you can significantly optimize the metabolic energy, mitochondrial function, and micro-environment of developing follicles during their final maturation window.

Here is an evidence-based medical breakdown of which supplements have clinical backing, how they work, and how we utilize them at our Nova IVF Fertility centers in Bengaluru.

The 90-Day Preconception Window: Why Timing Is Everything

Many women start taking supplements just 10 days before their egg retrieval and wonder why there is no difference in their egg numbers. In human ovarian biology, a primordial follicle takes approximately 90 to 120 days to journey from a resting microscopic pool to an ovulatory follicle.

During this critical three-to-four-month pre-antral phase, developing oocytes are actively replicating mitochondria and synthesizing cellular ATP. This is the physiological window where targeted nutraceutical priming has real clinical impact.

CoQ10 (Ubiquinol): Recharging the Cellular Batteries

The Mitochondrial Mechanism

The human oocyte is the most mitochondria-dense cell in the entire human body, containing up to 200,000 mitochondria. Coenzyme Q10 is an essential electron carrier in the mitochondrial respiratory chain. After age 35, endogenous CoQ10 levels plummet, starving the egg of energy needed for meiotic spindle alignment.

Clinical Dosage & Form

We recommend Ubiquinol (the pre-converted, active antioxidant form of CoQ10) at 400 mg to 600 mg daily with a fat-containing meal for at least 60 to 90 days before ovarian stimulation. Studies confirm higher fertilization rates and lower embryo aneuploidy.

DHEA: Restoring the Ovarian Micro-Environment

Dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA) is a mild androgen precursor produced by the adrenal glands and ovaries. In ovaries with diminished reserve, follicular granulosa cells suffer from an androgen-deficient microenvironment, leading to accelerated follicular death (atresia).

• How It Helps: DHEA increases intra-ovarian IGF-1 (Insulin-like Growth Factor 1) and up-regulates FSH receptor sensitivity on growing follicles, helping rescue follicles that would otherwise perish.
• Who Benefits: Women with Low AMH (< 1.0 ng/mL) and Poor Ovarian Response.
• Important Caution: DHEA must never be taken blindly without testing baseline DHEA-S and total testosterone. If levels are already elevated (as in PCOS), taking DHEA can impair egg quality and trigger acne and hair loss.

Other Evidence-Based Supplements

  • Vitamin D3 (with K2): Vitamin D acts like a steroid hormone in the ovary and endometrium. Optimizing serum levels (>40 ng/mL) improves IVF clinical pregnancy rates.
  • Active L-Methylfolate (5-MTHF): Essential for cellular DNA methylation and homocysteine reduction, particularly in Indian patients who carry the MTHFR gene mutation.
  • Omega-3 Fatty Acids (EPA/DHA): High-purity fish oils reduce systemic pelvic inflammation and improve uterine artery blood flow.
  • Melatonin (3 mg at bedtime): A potent intra-follicular free-radical scavenger that shields oocytes from oxidative stress during stimulation.

Doctor's Advice

Supplements are not a substitute for an expert IVF protocol, but when strategically introduced 3 months before your cycle, they provide your eggs with the best possible metabolic support.

Always review your supplement regimen with your reproductive specialist to ensure personalized dosages and avoid unnecessary or counter-productive interactions.
