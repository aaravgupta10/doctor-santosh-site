DuoStim Protocol in IVF: How Double Ovarian Stimulation Maximizes Embryos for Low AMH

By Dr. Santosh Gupta November 18, 2026

For women diagnosed with Diminished Ovarian Reserve (DOR) or an Anti-Müllerian Hormone (AMH) level below 1.0 ng/mL, the traditional 'one stimulation per month' model can feel frustratingly slow. In conventional IVF, patients undergo ovarian stimulation, egg retrieval, and must then wait one or two cycles before attempting another retrieval.

Time is the most critical resource in reproductive medicine. To maximize the number of viable embryos while minimizing biological delay, international reproductive centers have adopted the DuoStim (Double Stimulation) protocol—an innovative approach that stimulates the ovaries twice within a single menstrual cycle.

At Nova IVF Fertility in Bengaluru, we customize DuoStim for poor ovarian responders, cancer patients requiring rapid fertility preservation, and women over 38 who need to bank chromosomally normal blastocysts quickly.

The Biological Breakthrough: Challenging the Single-Wave Follicular Dogma

For decades, medical textbooks taught that a cohort of follicles grows only once a month during the follicular phase (the first two weeks of the menstrual cycle). Recent ultrasonic and endocrinological research has disproven this, confirming that human ovaries recruit multiple follicular waves continuously—even during the luteal phase (after ovulation).

DuoStim capitalizes on this biological reality. Instead of allowing luteal phase follicles to undergo natural atresia (degeneration), we administer a second round of gonadotropin stimulation just 2 to 5 days after the first egg retrieval.

Clinical Efficiency

By executing follicular phase stimulation (FPS) and luteal phase stimulation (LPS) back-to-back within roughly 28 to 30 days, patients achieve the cumulative embryo yield of two full IVF cycles in less than half the calendar time.

Step-by-Step: How a DuoStim Cycle Works

First Wave (Follicular Phase)

Gonadotropin stimulation begins on Day 2 or 3 of menses. Follicles are monitored via transvaginal ultrasound and estradiol tracking until maturity.

First Ovum Pick-Up (OPU 1)

Triggered with a GnRH agonist to prevent OHSS. Eggs are fertilized via ICSI and cultured to Day 5/6 blastocysts, then vitrified (flash-frozen).

Second Wave (Luteal Phase)

Just 2 to 5 days post-OPU 1, regardless of bleeding, a second round of gonadotropin injections begins, targeting the newly recruited follicular wave.

Second Ovum Pick-Up (OPU 2)

Mature follicles are retrieved, fertilized, cultured to blastocyst stage, and pooled with the first batch. All embryos remain frozen for a future FET.

Who Benefits Most from DuoStim in Bengaluru?

  • Women with Low AMH (<1.0 ng/mL) or Low Antral Follicle Count (AFC <5): When a patient yields only 2 to 4 eggs per retrieval, pooling eggs across two back-to-back waves dramatically improves the statistical probability of obtaining a euploid (genetically balanced) embryo.
  • Women Aged 37 to 43: Biological age correlates with increased chromosomal aneuploidy in eggs. DuoStim allows rapid accumulation of blastocysts before cumulative age-related decline accelerates.
  • Prior Suboptimal IVF Responses: Patients who previously produced empty follicles or poor-quality eggs during the follicular phase frequently exhibit better follicle synchronization and egg quality during the luteal phase stimulation.
  • Pre-Cancer / Oncological Fertility Preservation: Women scheduled for urgent chemotherapy or pelvic radiation who have only a 3-week window to preserve genetic material.

Are Luteal Phase Eggs as Healthy as Follicular Phase Eggs?

One of the most frequent questions patients ask Dr. Santosh Gupta is whether eggs retrieved during the luteal phase suffer from inferior developmental potential. High-level clinical trials published in leading reproductive journals—including the European Society of Human Reproduction and Embryology (ESHRE)—have conclusively proven that fertilization rates, blastocyst conversion rates, and euploidy rates are identical between follicular and luteal stimulations.

In fact, many clinical studies demonstrate slightly higher mature (MII) oocyte recovery during the luteal wave because progesterone levels naturally suppress premature LH surges, allowing more homogeneous follicular synchronization.
